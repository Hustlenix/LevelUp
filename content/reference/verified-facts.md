# Verified Facts — THE ONLY ALLOWED SOURCE OF EXTERNAL CLAIMS

Every study, number, grade, and protocol below is verified. Chapters may reference these and
nothing else. Grades: **A** verified science · **B** legitimate framework · **C** plausible but
unverified · **D** pseudoscience/misattribution.

## Studies & claims (claim id → grade → verdict)

### Belief & placebo
- **C-1 Housekeepers study — A.** Crum & Langer (2007), *Psychological Science* 18(2):165–171.
  84 female hotel room attendants told their work met the Surgeon General's definition of
  exercise lost weight, lowered BMI, waist-to-hip ratio, and blood pressure in 4 weeks without
  objectively changing behavior; perceived exercise rose ~39%→79%. Mechanism: expectation,
  subtle effort/posture/stress changes — a modulator, not magic. Not proof that belief alone
  produces material outcomes without action.
- **C-2 Milkshake study — A.** Crum, Corbin, Brownell & Salovey (2011), *Health Psychology*
  30(4):424–429. Same 380-calorie shake labeled "indulgent" (620 cal) vs "sensible" (140 cal):
  the indulgent label produced a steeper ghrelin (hunger hormone) decline, n=46. Mindset →
  physiology, measurable.
- **C-3 Open-label placebo — A.** Kaptchuk et al. (2010), *PLoS ONE* 5(12):e15591. Placebo pills
  explicitly labeled inactive still improved IBS symptoms vs no treatment. Replicated in chronic
  low back pain (Carvalho et al., 2016, *Pain*) and a 2021 meta-analysis. Expectation, ritual,
  and meaning drive much of placebo — no deception needed.
- **C-4 Power posing — D (physiology) / B (felt state).** Carney, Cuddy & Yap (2010),
  *Psychological Science* 21(10):1363–1368 reported testosterone +20%, cortisol −25%, risk-taking
  ↑ after 2 minutes. Ranehill et al. (2015, *Psychological Science*, n=200) replication found NO
  hormone/risk effects — only self-reported feelings of power. Simonsohn (2017) p-curve said the
  evidence was too weak; Carney publicly disavowed strong claims. Safe phrasing: posture shifts
  how powerful you *feel*, briefly; not your biology.
- **C-5 RAS 11M bits — D (numbers) / B (filter).** "11 million vs 50 bits per second" is an
  estimate from Tor Norretranders, *The User Illusion* (1998), not a measured study. RAS is a
  real brainstem structure; attention filtering is real (C-6). Drop the numerology, keep the
  filter concept.
- **C-6 Gorilla experiment — A.** Simons & Chabris (1999), *Perception* 28(9):1059–1074. ~50% of
  observers missed a person in a gorilla suit while counting basketball passes. Inattentional
  blindness is one of the most replicated findings in psychology. This is the true basis of
  "what you focus on, you find."
- **C-7 Mind-wandering — A (data) / D (inference).** Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010), *Science*
  330(6006):932. ~250,000 iPhone samples from ~2,250 adults: minds wander 46.9% of the time and
  wandering predicts unhappiness — whether the drift is past OR future. The training claim
  "thinking about the future makes you happier" inverts the finding. Keep "train attention";
  drop "fantasize about the future."
- **C-8 Mental practice 35% vs 53% — A (accurate, domain-limited).** Ranganathan et al. (2004),
  *Neuropsychologia* 42(7):944–956. 12 weeks of imagined pinky-finger abduction → +35% strength
  vs +53% physical training. Founding study: Yue & Cole (1992), *Journal of Neurophysiology*
  67(5):1114–1123, 22% mental vs 30% physical. Motor imagery activates much of the same cortex as
  execution. Scope: strength/skill — do not extend to "imagine wealth, attract wealth."
- **C-9 Process vs outcome visualization — A (finding) / C (attribution).** Pham & Taylor (1999),
  *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 25(2):250–260 — UCLA, NOT Stanford, and not NLP.
  Students who simulated the *process* of studying (planning, scheduling, doing) studied more and
  got better grades than students who simulated the outcome. One of the most useful findings here.
- **C-10 Bannister 4-minute mile — B.** Facts verified: May 6, 1954, Roger Bannister ran 3:59.4
  at Oxford; Gunder Hägg's 4:01.4 (1945) had stood 9 years; John Landy broke 4 minutes 46 days
  later. "Psychological barrier" is a fair interpretation but training, pacing, and pacesetters
  also improved. Barriers fall when preparation meets opportunity — belief plus preparation.

### Practice, habit, energy
- **C-11 10,000 hours — B (framework) / C (the number).** Deliberate practice is real (Ericsson);
  meta-analysis Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014), *Psychological Science* 25(8):1608–1618:
  practice explains 26% of variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, <1% in
  professions. "10,000 hours" is a heuristic, not a threshold.
- **C-12 70% rule — A (doctrine).** Real USMC doctrine for decisions under uncertainty (The Basic
  School), popularized by David Freedman's *Corps Business* (2000); Jeff Bezos uses the same logic.
  Act at 70% rather than waiting for certainty.
- **C-13 No zero days — B.** Phrase originates from a 2013 Reddit post (r/getdisciplined, user
  ryans01), not a study. As a behavioral rule it aligns with habit formation and behavioral
  activation. Real origin, real utility, zero research pedigree.
- **C-14 Behavioral activation — A.** Dimidjian et al. (2006), *Journal of Consulting and Clinical
  Psychology* 74(4):658–670 (JCCP — NOT JAMA): 241 adults with major depression; behavioral
  activation (schedule meaningful activities, act first) was comparable to antidepressant
  medication and outperformed cognitive therapy in severe depression. COBRA trial (Lancet, 2016)
  confirmed non-inferiority to CBT; a 2019 reanalysis contested the severity-moderator result.
  "Action precedes and generates motivation" is among the best-supported ideas in the corpus.
- **C-15 Growth mindset — B.** Sisk et al. (2018), *Psychological Science* 29(4):549–571: two
  meta-analyses; mindset–achievement r ≈ .10; intervention d ≈ .08 overall, d ≈ .19 for high-risk
  students. Real but modest — a small lever, often oversold.
- **C-16 Ego depletion — D (model) / B (experience).** The flagship effect failed a 23-lab
  preregistered replication: Hagger et al. (2016), *Perspectives on Psychological Science*
  11(4):546–573, n=2,141, d = 0.04. Mental fatigue is real; "limited fuel tank" is not the
  mechanism. Beliefs about willpower predict the experience (Job & Dweck).
- **C-17 Parole judges / decision fatigue — B/C.** Danziger, Levav & Avnaim-Pesso (2011), *PNAS*
  108(17):6889–6892: 1,112 parole decisions by 8 Israeli judges; favorable rulings fell ~65% →
  ~0% across a session, rebounding after breaks. BUT case order was not random (Weinshall-Margel
  & Shapard, 2011, *PNAS*), effect magnitude likely overstated (Glöckner, 2016), and a 2025
  registered report found no effect in physicians. "Decision quality degrades over long sessions"
  is real across domains; the headline numbers are contested.
- **C-18 Sleep — B (direction) / C (framing).** Dawson & Reid (1997), *Nature* 388(6639):235:
  17h awake ≈ BAC 0.05%; ~24h ≈ 0.10%. Williamson & Feyer (2000), *Occupational and Environmental
  Medicine* 57(10):649–655: 17–19h awake ≈ 0.05%. This is about SUSTAINED wakefulness, not a
  6-hour night. Direction solid: sleep loss degrades cognition, mood, self-control.
- **C-19 Interruptions 23 min — B/C.** Gloria Mark et al. (2008), CHI '08, "The Cost of
  Interrupted Work": interruptions genuinely derail tasks; interrupted workers compensate by
  working FASTER with more stress and frustration. The famous "23 minutes 15 seconds to refocus"
  comes from Mark's follow-up interviews, not the paper. Protect focus blocks; drop the exact
  number.
- **C-20 Dopamine detox — D (mechanism) / B (behavior).** The protocol derives from UCSF
  psychiatrist Cameron Sepah's CBT protocol (stimulus control + exposure-response prevention) for
  compulsive tech use — legitimate. There is NO evidence fasting resets receptors or baseline
  dopamine. Anna Lembke's *Dopamine Nation* (2021) is the reputable version: homeostasis,
  pleasure–pain balance, abstinence as pragmatic reset. Keep the behavior, drop the neuro-story.
- **C-21 Flow 5x — D (claim) / B (flow).** "McKinsey study: executives 5x more productive in
  flow" traces to Steven Kotler's *The Rise of Superman* (2014) citing a ten-year McKinsey study
  that has never been published or located. Flow is a real, well-documented state
  (Csikszentmihalyi); challenge–skill balance genuinely improves engagement. Quote flow's
  psychology, never the 5x.
- **C-22 Zeigarnik — D (memory claim) / B (motivational claim).** Zeigarnik's 1927 "interrupted
  tasks remembered ~90% better" has replicated poorly (her own students: <1/3 of 44 attempts;
  van Bergen 1968 exact replication failed; Ghibellini & Meier 2025 meta-analysis found no memory
  advantage). What DOES replicate: the pull to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect) and
  unfulfilled goals intruding on attention until discharged or PLANNED (Masicampo & Baumeister,
  2011, *JPSP* 100(5):810–826). Advice stands; citation upgrades.

### Motivation, business stories, metaphysics
- **C-23 Buffett 500 pages — C (number) / B (habit).** "500 pages a day" traces to Todd Combs's
  2013 recollection of Buffett's advice to Columbia students (~2002); a 2010 WSJ profile says 500
  pages a WEEK; Quote Investigator couldn't verify the daily figure. Verified: Buffett reports
  5–6 hours of reading daily and frames knowledge as compounding.
- **C-24 Pat Flynn $8k — B.** Verified: ~$7,900–8,000 in October 2008 (first month monetizing his
  blog) — but the blog existed ~2 years before that with no income. First-year total ~$200k,
  documented in published income reports. The "overnight" framing hides years of unpaid groundwork.
- **C-25 Negative self-talk 40–50x — C (direction) / D (the ratio).** Negativity bias is real:
  Baumeister et al. (2001), *Review of General Psychology* 5(4):323–370 reviewed 200+ studies —
  bad events/feedback/emotions outweigh good ones of equal magnitude. Ito et al. (1998) found
  larger brain responses to negative images. But no study supports a 40–50x ratio; the number is
  stage-talk (Moawad/Ed Mylett podcast) with no primary source. Gottman's 5:1 ratio applies only
  to marital interaction; the Fredrickson–Losada 3:1 ratio was RETRACTED (Brown, Sokal & Friedman,
  2013).
- **C-26 Subtraction bias — A.** Adams, Converse, Hales & Klotz (2021), *Nature* 592(7853):258–
  261: across 8 experiments people default to ADDING elements when improving systems, even when
  subtracting is better. Leidy Klotz's *Subtract* (2021) is the practitioner book. One of the most
  robust modern findings in the corpus.
- **C-27 Theta & suggestibility — B/C.** Sabourin, Cutcomb, Crawford & Pribram (1990),
  *International Journal of Psychophysiology* 10(2):125–147: highly hypnotizable subjects showed
  greater theta power; theta increased during hypnosis. Direction supported; "programming before
  age 7" and theta-as-gateway go beyond the evidence.
- **C-28 Vacuum principle — D (as physics) / B (as psychology).** No mechanism by which an empty
  space attracts outcomes. Useful residue is well supported: non-attachment to outcomes improves
  decision quality and reduces desperate behavior (scarcity degrades judgment — Mullainathan &
  Shafir); goals direct attention (C-5, C-6). Use the psychology; discard the cosmology.
- **C-29 Lag / balance / mirror — B (lag) / C (balance) / C (mirror).** The LAG is real:
  compounding processes (skill, audience, wealth) show delayed visible returns; the J-curve is
  standard. "Balance is a lie" is a strategy claim (depth beats hedging) — a values judgment, not
  a finding. The "mirror" (everything reflects your state) is confirmation bias wearing a costume:
  the mind filters for what it believes, so the world appears to reflect your state. Useful as a
  self-audit prompt; false as ontology.
- **C-30 Hate your life — B (action) / D (framing).** Evidence-compatible core is behavioral
  activation (C-14): act first, mood follows. The self-attacking framing is unnecessary and
  contrary to evidence — self-attack is a documented risk factor, not fuel. Correct framing:
  "treat your situation as unacceptable and act accordingly."
