## Overview

You have heard the advice "fake it till you make it," and you have probably noticed it is both true and dangerous. This chapter repairs it. The honest version is that you do not have to believe it yet. You can borrow the posture, the pace and the phrasing of someone who does. Use their state as a bridge into action. Then repay the loan with repetition until the state is genuinely yours. Borrowed states, owned eventually.

The mechanism is deliberately built from the body upward, and one piece of honest science sits at its centre. The famous power-posing results — the hormones, the risk-taking — did not survive replication. What survived is felt power. Brief, self-reported, but real enough to change the next five seconds. And five seconds is the size of a decision. This chapter builds a practice on what is actually left standing. It is more useful than what collapsed.

## 01 — What a borrowed state is

States — confidence, calm, urgency, warmth — are usually treated as weather. They arrive and pass. You act inside whatever weather you woke up with. The trainings make a different claim. States are switchable. The switch sits on the lower floors of the stack from Chapter 8. Body and breath. The environmental layer, not the spiritual one.

When your own state-library lacks the entry you need, you borrow. You do not summon the feeling from nothing. You copy its exterior from someone who has it. Posture, breathing rhythm, pace of speech, phrasing. The outward signs are all the body has of a state. The body reads them in others, so it reads them in you. This is acting, in the technical sense. It is available to you the moment you choose a model instead of waiting for a mood.

## 02 — The body enters first

Here is the required honesty, stated plainly, because this chapter's credibility lives on it. In 2010, a famous experiment reported that two minutes of expansive posing raised testosterone by about twenty per cent. It lowered cortisol by a quarter. It increased risk-taking. The replication in 2015 — two hundred participants — found no hormone changes and no risk differences. A p-curve analysis judged the original evidence too weak. The lead author of the original publicly walked back the strong claims. You should read that as settled: posture does not change your biology.

What did replicate is felt power. People who adopted the posture reported feeling more powerful, briefly. That is not nothing. Feeling powerful for five seconds is a five-second window in which the decision — the call, the raise, the first line of the talk — becomes reachable. Breath works the same way on the first line. A slow exhale is the fastest state tool you own. It is the one you have in any room, any time. The body enters first. The state follows the body. The action follows the state.

## 03 — Borrowing belief from your future self

The borrowed state gets a second engine when you combine it with rehearsal. The Identity Stack and the schedules of the version of you who already did the thing — Chapter 3's move — give you a specific model to borrow from. Your own future self. The one for whom the hard moment is already history. You borrow their posture and their pace.

And because mental rehearsal trains real execution — the motor-imagery machinery from Chapter 7 — borrowing with rehearsal stops being pretending. Rehearsing the first thirty seconds of the hard conversation in the borrowed role is practice. The same cortex activates for imagined and performed execution. Borrowing plus rehearsal equals training. Training is what pays down the loan.

## 04 — The repayment plan

A borrowed state is a loan, and the loan has terms. Repetition plus real behaviour converts the borrowed state into an owned one. This is the best-supported mechanism in the whole stack: behavioural activation. Act first, and the feeling follows. You borrow confidence. You take the action the confident person would take. The action produces evidence. The evidence is what the next borrow rests on. Until eventually you do not need to borrow at all.

Borrowed swallows are fine. Borrowed identity is impostorism. If you only ever perform the exterior without doing the bridge behaviours — the calls, the workouts, the drafts, the uncomfortable questions — the loan never converts. You live permanently in the feeling of being found out. The repayment plan is concrete. Every day, do the behaviours the state is meant to serve. Log what they let you start. The stack of repaid loans is your actual confidence.

## 05 — When borrowing goes wrong

Two errors break the practice. First, borrowing from a fantasy person. The imago of a movie character. A composite of "what confident people are like." That gives you a model with no real movement in it. Borrow from a real archive. Someone you know. A documented case. A person whose recorded behaviour you can actually imitate. Real people have flaws in their posture. Those flaws are part of what makes their state achievable. Fantasy people have no posture at all.

Second, never use the borrowed state to skip preparation. The state buys you the start. The first thirty seconds. The first sentence. The first rep. It does not buy the outcome. It never replaces the work the outcome requires. Borrow the state to begin. The beginning is where the borrowed state earns its keep.

## Key Ideas

- States are switchable via body and environment — postures, pace, breath, phrasing.
- Felt power is real; the hormonal power-posing claims did not replicate — be honest about both.
- Five seconds of borrowed state is the size of the decision the state is for.
- Borrowing plus rehearsal is practice: imagery trains the same cortex as execution.
- The loan converts to ownership through repetition and real behaviour — act first, feel follows.
- Borrow from real people, never fantasy, and never to skip preparation.

## Apply Today

1. Choose one real model for the state you need — a person you know, or a documented case, not a fantasy.
2. Tomorrow, enter your hard moment in their posture and pace: shoulders, breath, walk, first words.
3. Rehearse the first thirty seconds of the moment tonight, in the borrowed role, in first person.
4. Log one thing the borrowed state let you start — evidence for the next borrow.

## The Science

Ranehill and colleagues (2015) attempted to replicate the 2010 power-posing findings with two hundred participants and found no hormonal changes and no risk-taking differences — only self-reported feelings of power; a p-curve analysis found the original evidence weak, and the original lead author disavowed the strong claims. What it licenses: posture shifts how powerful you feel, briefly. What it does not license: any claim that posture alters testosterone, cortisol or risk behaviour.

Dimidjian and colleagues (2006) found behavioural activation comparable to antidepressant medication for major depression and superior to cognitive therapy in severe cases. What it licenses: acting first generates motivation — the repayment mechanism for borrowed states. What it does not license: waiting to feel ready before acting.

Ranganathan and colleagues (2004) showed twelve weeks of imagined practice produced thirty-five per cent strength gains versus fifty-three per cent physical. What it licenses: rehearsal in the borrowed role is genuine practice for execution. What it does not license: rehearsal substituting for execution.
