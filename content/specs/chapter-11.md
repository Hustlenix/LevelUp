## Sections
**Overview** — The trap: wanting everything at once = static; wanting from lack = scramble.
This chapter dismantles the metaphysics of "want less and it comes" and rebuilds it as
signal hygiene.

**01 — The cluttered receiver** — Metaphor: a radio dial set to ten stations at once. The
desire-signal from Ch 5 requires one strong, specific signal. Attention evidence: your filter
admits what your goal structure primes (gorilla). Cluttered desire = filter tuned to noise —
you can't notice the opportunity you haven't specified.

**02 — The vacuum, honestly** — The original claim ("want nothing and the universe fills the
space") — no mechanism, untestable. The residue that survives: (a) desire without desperation;
(b) bandwidth freed by dropping low-grade wants; (c) decision quality improves under clarity.
The vacuum is not an empty life; it's a cleared channel.

**03 — Scarcity degrades the receiver** — Mullainathan & Shafir: scarcity captures attention
and degrades judgment — the mind tunneling on what's missing makes worse decisions about it.
Desperation ("I need ANY client right now") is a signal-scrambler. Calibrate want: strong
direction, free timing.

**04 — Wanting correctly** — The protocol: one primary want per season, written vividly; its
bridge actions scheduled; result-timing released (Vacuum Protocol). Wanting with your whole
attention — obsession's lighter sibling (Ch 10).

**05 — The trap's exit** — Audit: name every want; rank by (a) alignment with mission, (b)
actionability; keep the top 1–2; consciously retire the rest for the season. Retired wants
aren't dead; they're queued.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: static vs signal; vacuum = bandwidth, not magic;
scarcity scrambles; one want per season; retired ≠ dead. Apply: run the want audit; write the
season's one want + its bridge; release the timing (Vacuum Protocol); notice one thing your
cleared filter admits this week.
