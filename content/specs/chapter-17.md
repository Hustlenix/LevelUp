## Sections
**Overview** — Overthinking as the mind trying to eliminate uncertainty by thinking — a tool
mismatch. Frame the chapter: not "think less," but "decide at the right resolution."

**01 — Why the loop runs** — The open-loop machinery (unfinished goals intrude; planning
discharges — Masicampo & Baumeister): an undecided question is a permanently open loop.
Completing = deciding + writing the next action, not solving the universe.

**02 — The 70% solution** — USMC doctrine: decisions under uncertainty are made at ~70%
information (taught at The Basic School; popularized in Freedman's Corps Business; Bezos
echoes it). Waiting for 95% is waiting for the competition's decision. Cheap reversibility →
decide earlier; expensive reversibility → still decide, with test-sized stakes.

**03 — Decision drift and the long session** — The parole-judge pattern (Danziger et al.:
favorable rulings dropped across long sessions then rebounded after breaks) — read honestly:
case order confounds, effect magnitude contested, but "decision quality degrades over long
sessions" replicates across domains. Practical: big decisions early; breaks; remove trivial
choice loads. Not "the brain is a fuel tank" (ego depletion failed replication — Ch 19
context). Never quote "65% to 0%."

**04 — The decision protocols** — Resolution ladder: (1) is this reversible? (2) can I test
it at small stakes? (3) decide at 70% and write the next action; (4) tollgate review dates
the revision. Sleep on it applies to irreversible, high-cost cases only.
Overthinking audit: track how many decisions the loop actually improves — count zeroes.

**05 — Attention as the antidote** — Overthinking is attention parked on the open loop;
the One-Task Rule + Winner's Loop give attention a place to be (Ch 25 preview). When the
loop reopens, write the next action — paper is cheaper than rumination.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: tool mismatch; decide at 70%; decide early, review
on schedule; plan-making closes loops; attention parking. Apply: pick one parked decision;
rate reversibility; take the 70% stance today; write the next action; move decision reviews
to mornings.
