## Sections
**Overview** — The training's claim: a life without purpose is not a life with problems, it is
a life with a missing engine — and pain becomes workable when it has a direction. Frankl as
the anchor (meaning as the survivable frame).

**01 — Pain without direction vs pain with it** — Difference between being lost and being on a
hard road: both hurt; one moves. Behavioral activation's structure: meaningful activity
outperforms waiting to feel better — action as the mood engine. "Purpose is not a luxury
feeling; it's a load-bearing beam."

**02 — The three-step discovery** — (1) Identity shift: stop asking "what do I want from life?",
ask "what does life need from me?" / "what did I used to lose hours to?" (2) Recovery: purpose
is recovered, not invented — childhood and deep-flow clues (what absorbed you before you were
managed?). (3) Sharpening: purpose must expire eventually — convert to a 12-month mission.

**03 — The obsession advantage** — Deliberate, single-pointed attention (Ch 14's depth principle
preview) — obsession as focus's older sibling, with the honest caveat: obsession amplifies
whatever it points at, including escape.

**04 — Purpose and the vacuum** — Non-attached action returns: purpose works best with the
Vacuum Protocol's detachment from outcome timing (the goal is yours; the schedule of results is
not). Ties to the lag (Ch 6): purpose is what keeps you loading under compression.

**05 — The downside honesty** — Purpose isn't a rescue from action; purpose discovered but not
acted on becomes the worst kind of static. Purpose as identity (Ch 8): verbs first.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: meaning deficit; discovery via recovery; 12-month
mission; obsession = focused attention; purpose demands verbs. Apply: run the Discovery
Protocol; write the mission on paper dated; pick one bridge action this week; Vacuum Protocol
the outcome schedule.
