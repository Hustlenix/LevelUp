## Sections
**Overview** — Opens with Earl Nightingale: "We become what we think about." Introduce the
self-fulfilling loop as the operating system of the series. Set up: reality isn't changed by
belief alone, but belief changes what you see, attempt, and persist at.

**01 — The filter you never chose** — The brain receives millions of bits; attention admits a
fraction (keep honest: the 11M/50-bit figure is folklore — say "estimates"). Gorilla experiment:
half of observers miss the gorilla. "You are not fighting the world; you are fighting your
filter." What you believe programs what your filter admits.

**02 — The self-fulfilling loop** — Belief → expectation → behavior → outcome → (confirms)
belief. Example from transcript: student believes "I'm bad at math" → stops trying → bad grade →
confirmation. Explain + note the loop runs in both directions; use it deliberately or it uses you.

**03 — The body believes** — Housekeepers study (real weight/BP change from re-interpreting
their work). Milkshake study (ghrelin responds to the label, not just the shake). Open-label
placebo. "The interpretation you place on effort changes what the effort produces." Creator's
line: "A belief is a thought you've thought so many times it became a fact to your brain."

**04 — What belief cannot do** — Honest boundary: belief is a modulator, not a creator. "Eat less,
move more" example — belief alone doesn't burn fat; it changes the behavior that does. Kaptchuk +
Crum show real, bounded, physiological effects, not physics suspensions.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: filter, loop, physiological proof, boundary. Apply:
audit one limiting belief, re-interpret one chore as training, run today's Morning Calibration,
notice one thing you miss.
