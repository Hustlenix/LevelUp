## Sections
**Overview** — Define "delusion" as the trainings use it: not denying facts, but refusing to let
current evidence set the ceiling. The boldest example: Bannister.

**01 — The barrier that was never there** — Hägg's 4:01.4 stood nine years. Top athletes were
within striking distance and didn't try. May 6, 1954: Bannister runs 3:59.4. Landy breaks it 46
days later. Then dozens. "The barrier was real in one place only — the ceiling athletes believed
in." Honest note: training, pacesetters, tactics also improved simultaneously; belief met
preparation.

**02 — Two ways to read 'delusion'** — Wrong way: believe against evidence, deny reality (danger,
inaction). Right way: hold the belief that the current evidence is not final. "The only person
who wins is the one who believed it first." Delusion = mental rehearsal with permission; the
Belief Audit is how you test it — act as if, then measure.

**03 — Borrowing belief before you own it** — You don't have to fully believe at day one; borrow
the posture of someone who does (preview of Chapter 9). Power posing honesty here: original
claims of hormone changes did not replicate; posture shifts how powerful you feel, briefly —
enough to start a sentence, a call, a rep.

**04 — The evidence problem** — You won't get evidence for a future you until you act from it.
The loop from Chapter 1 means your filter only shows evidence matching your belief. So pick the
belief; the data will follow the behavior.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: barrier is permission-shaped; delusion is refusal, not
denial; borrow belief; evidence follows action. Apply: name one ceiling you inherited, find one
person who did it, act at 70% scale today, schedule a Belief Audit.
