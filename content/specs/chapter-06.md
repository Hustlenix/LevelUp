## Sections
**Overview** — The training teaches that most real change happens below the visible surface:
repeated loading with no visible movement, then sudden phase change. Introduce hysteresis
carefully as a material-science analogy, not a claim about brains.

**01 — Hysteresis, plainly** — What hysteresis actually is (a material's response remembers its
loading history; used in magnets, shape-memory alloys). The analogy: your progress curve
remembers every work block, even the ones that showed nothing. "You are not stuck; you are
compressing."

**02 — Compression and phase change** — Water at 99°C looks like water at 30°C until one more
degree. Latent heat = energy absorbed with no temperature rise. Relate: the invisible decade of
skill-building that precedes the "sudden" expert. Practice variance honesty here (Macnamara):
compression is real, uniform payout is not.

**03 — The danger of the missing middle** — When nothing visibly moves, the untrained mind reads
"it's not working" and switches plans (strategy surfing). This is when most people quit or
scatter. The Lag Reframe exists precisely for this window.

**04 — Loop hygiene in the unseen phase** — The motivational pull of open loops (resume effect;
planning discharges the intrusion — Masicampo & Baumeister). During flat phases, close small
loops daily: visible checkmarks are the architecture's scaffolding, not decoration.

**05 — Reading your own curve** — Leading indicators (inputs) vs lagging indicators (outputs).
During compression, review inputs daily, outputs monthly; don't renegotiate strategy on a
flat month.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: hysteresis analogy; phase change; quit-proofing the flat
phase; loop hygiene; input vs output review. Apply: pick one area you judge "stuck" and list
three inputs; add one daily closed loop; write a note to your future self at the next phase
change; schedule The Lag Reframe.
