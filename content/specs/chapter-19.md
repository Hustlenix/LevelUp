## Sections
**Overview** — The inversion that unlocks everything: the corpus's best-supported idea is that
action precedes and generates motivation (behavioral activation), so waiting for the feeling
is waiting on a condition you control in reverse.

**01 — Motivation is generated, not found** — Behavioral activation evidence (Dimidjian et al.:
scheduling meaningful actions outperformed waiting and thoughts-first approaches; the JAMA
attribution is wrong — it's JCCP). Practical law: motivation = f(latest reps). The first rep
is cold by design; warmth comes on reps 2–10.

**02 — Should-language audit** — "I should go to the gym" fuels the loop of guilt without
action. Diagnosis: shoulds are unowned goals — either convert to "I choose, at this time" or
delete the should. The should-exercise: rewrite three shoulds today.

**03 — Momentum mechanics** — Smallest possible wins as pacemakers (2-minute rule carryover;
Winner's Loop synergy). Momentum beats mood: a single 70%-scale rep is worth more than an
evening of ideal planning (70% rule: act before readiness).

**04 — Weather professionalism** — Professionals treat motivation as weather: they don't
postpone flights for clouds. The pro protocol: observer's log (rate motivation 1–10 daily;
notice it correlates with LAST day's reps, not inspiration); morning calibration sets
interpretation before the first rep; floor rules cover low-motivation days.

**05 — The honest ceiling** — Motivation work doesn't replace clarity (aim) or systems
(repetition); a motivated person pointed nowhere is a faster carousel horse. Sequence: aim
(Ch 4), then reps, then motivation as the byproduct meter.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: motivation is output; shoulds are unowned goals;
momentum over mood; weather professionalism; aim before reps. Apply: one cold first rep
today; convert three shoulds; start the 1–10 motivation log; schedule tomorrow's first rep
in the calendar.
