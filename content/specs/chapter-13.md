## Sections
**Overview** — The provocative title and the honest repair: discontent as information. The
original framing (self-attack as fuel) is both unnecessary and contrary to the evidence; the
repair (circumstances are unacceptable → act) keeps everything useful.

**01 — The pain threshold story** — The trainings' observation: people with a high "I'll act
when it hurts enough" threshold tolerate escalating discomfort instead of changing. The
insight that survives: thresholds are not fixed; they respond to reframing. Mapping the
personal threshold: which problems have you accepted that you'd call unacceptable if they
happened to a friend?

**02 — Discontent is data, not instruction** — Dissatisfaction = signal of misalignment
(Chapter 1's filter at work). What it must NOT become: self-contempt. Self-attack is a
documented risk factor, not a motivational device. "The mirror must be aimed at the
circumstance, never at the person in it."

**03 — Action before feeling** — Behavioral activation: scheduling meaningful action precedes
and generates the mood that makes action easier — the reverse of "wait until you feel like
it." Hate-as-fuel works only through the bridge (Ch 5): anger becomes outline → draft → call.

**04 — The upper limit problem** — Hendricks: people unconsciously cap their results to match
their self-image of deservingness (the thermostat). The cap shows up as self-sabotage when
things go RIGHT. Raising the ceiling: rehearse ownership (Ch 7's technique), accept
discomfort of bigger room. Framework grade B — honest.

**05 — The correct formula** — "Treat the current situation as unacceptable and act
accordingly, at 70% scale, starting today." Discontent → specificity → bridge action.
Summarize the chapter's reframe in one line.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: thresholds are reframable; discontent = signal;
self-attack banned; action precedes mood; raise the thermostat. Apply: list three accepted
problems as if a friend's; convert the worst into a Discovery Protocol goal; take the first
70% step today; log one upper-limit moment this week.
