## Sections
**Overview** — Most plans are forwards: "do X, hope for Y." The backwards equation starts at the
finish line and works the path back to today. Origin framing: grand strategists (Napoleon,
Alexander) planned operations from the objective backward — keep this as color, not citation.

**01 — Define the ending first** — The ending must be specific, dated, and measurable — "revenue
of X by Y," not "be successful." Vagueness is the first cheat code of the comfort zone.

**02 — Work the path backward** — From the ending, ask: what has to be true six months out? Three
months? Thirty days? This week? Today? Each answer becomes a milestone file. "If you can't name
step seven, it's not a plan."

**03 — Tollgates, not checkpoints** — Tollgates = recurring alignment reviews: is the path still
the path, or are we optimizing toward the wrong summit? (Creator's term.) Agenda: results vs
plan, assumptions tested, one adjustment max per gate. Plan is a hypothesis, not a promise.

**04 — Simulate the steps, not the victory** — Pham & Taylor: students who simulated the process
outperformed outcome simulators. Why: outcome fantasy supplies finish-line feelings; process
simulation builds real preparation. This is the science spine of the chapter.

**05 — The honest ceiling** — Deliberate practice matters, but its payoff varies by domain
(Macnamara: 26% variance in games, ~4% education, <1% professions). Backwards planning assumes
the path exists; some summits have no path — tollgates catch that too.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: ending first; backwards milestones; tollgates; process
simulation; plan = hypothesis. Apply: write the dated ending; decompose to 20 steps; schedule
first step today; book a tollgate in 30 days.
