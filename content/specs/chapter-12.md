## Sections
**Overview** — Water metaphor: laminar (smooth, directed) vs turbulent (chaotic, dissipating)
flow. Effortless = aligned + directed, not absent. Flow as the laminar mode of work.

**01 — The alignment loop** — The trainings' loop: LIGHTEN (drop what doesn't matter — Ch 26
preview), LEVEL (match challenge to skill), LEAN (one thing, fully). Each step maps to
evidence: challenge–skill balance is the core finding of flow research (Csikszentmihalyi).

**02 — What flow is (and isn't)** — Flow is a real, documented state: deep absorption, merged
action-awareness, distorted time. What it ISN'T: a proven 5x productivity multiplier — that
"McKinsey study" has never been published; drop it entirely from the chapter.

**03 — Engineering the conditions** — The environment does the engineering: one task (Ch 25),
no interruption sources (Mark et al.: interruptions really derail; interrupted workers work
faster and more stressed), a clear objective, immediate feedback on progress. Flow is
scheduled, not awaited.

**04 — The wandering mind at the door** — Minds wander ~47% of the time and wandering
correlates with unhappiness (Killingsworth & Gilbert) — the honest hook for why absorption
feels good. Flow isn't euphoria; it's attention finally parked.

**05 — The day's laminar block** — The Flow Alignment Loop protocol: difficulty-rated task
list; task ≈ skill +1; schedule in the freshest 90 minutes; protect from notifications;
log the day's flow signature (the task you lost track of time on) — then schedule more of it.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: laminar vs turbulent; align, don't push; schedule flow;
protect the block; no 5x claims. Apply: rate tomorrow's tasks 1–10 by difficulty and skill;
pick the +1 task; timebox it into the freshest block; remove two interruption sources;
log today's flow signature.
