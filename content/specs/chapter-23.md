## Sections
**Overview** — Reframe: consistency is not a perfect streak; it's average comeback speed.
Procrastination is not laziness; it's an open-loop tax you pay every day it compounds.

**01 — Comeback speed** — The measure that matters: hours (not days) between miss and return.
Evidence family: open loops intrude until discharged (Masicampo & Baumeister — the
motivational pull; the "90% memory" Zeigarnik claim itself failed replication — be honest
and cite the modern mechanism). Misses are data, not identity (Ch 15's lines: a missed day
dips the orange, not the green).

**02 — The identity anchor** — Why streaks break identities: "I'm not a consistent person"
reads the miss as verdict (Ch 1's loop). The anchor: the zero-day floor and "never two"
rules (Ch 18) plus identity-vote framing — one abstention doesn't cancel the election.

**03 — The antifragile loop** — Designed repair: miss → scheduled catch-up slot (not a
marathon) → review of the cause (input, not excuse) → one system tweak. The loop turns
failures into system improvements — that's the antifragility: each break makes the next
streak longer.

**04 — The procrastination cost table** — What postponement actually costs: compounding
interest against you (the J-curve inverted: debt also compounds), the open-loop tax on
attention (plan it or it intrudes), and the identity vote cast by choosing "later" (Ch 8).
Decide: is this a task to shrink (Ch 26) or a task to start (Ch 18's 2-minute)?

**05 — Consistency is a system, not a mood** — The weekly consistency audit: floors met?
comeback times? one tweak? Consistency compounds through the lag (Ch 6): the flat phase is
where the consistent pull ahead — this is the mechanism behind every "sudden success."

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: comeback speed; anchors over streaks; antifragile
repair; open-loop tax; consistency as system. Apply: log today's comeback time; write the
catch-up slot; schedule one system tweak; plan or delete one open loop.
