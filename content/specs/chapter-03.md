## Sections
**Overview** — The trainings' claim: "every version of you already exists; attention collapses
the one that becomes real." State plainly: borrowed from quantum physics as metaphor; a possible
self is a trajectory, not a parallel reality. The useful truth: who you become is selected by
repeated attention + behavior.

**01 — The metaphor and its limits** — How superposition actually works in physics vs what the
trainings claim. "The universe doesn't need a parallel one — it needs your next 90 days." Keep
the poetry, name the physics honestly.

**02 — Attention is the collapse mechanism** — Gorilla experiment: what you're counting
determines what you see. Same brain, same day — different self depending on where attention
goes. The version of you that already did the thing is available the moment your filter admits
evidence of it.

**03 — The version that already did it** — Creator's device: "How would the version of you who
already achieved X spend tomorrow morning?" Answering daily = acting from the future self's
schedule. Support: process visualization beats outcome fantasy — imagine the steps, not the
pride (Pham & Taylor; note: UCLA study, commonly misattributed to Stanford).

**04 — Collapse is a vote count** — Identity change = repeated small collapses (attention +
behavior) — preview of identity votes in Ch 8/18. Each decision collapses dozens of possible
yous. You don't discover the future self; you build it by selection.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: metaphor vs mechanics; attention selects; future-self
interview; votes accumulate. Apply: write tomorrow's schedule as the achieved version of you;
run Morning Calibration; count today's votes; one decision made as the future self.
