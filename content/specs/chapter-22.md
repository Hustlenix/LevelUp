## Sections
**Overview** — The law: 1 hour/day on ONE compounding skill (or knowledge domain) — the
learner's life. Small, boring, unstoppable. What it is NOT: an overnight mastery claim.

**01 — The hour, defined** — Rules of the hour: one domain per season (Ch 11's tuning);
first 90-minute freshest block if possible (Ch 12's laminar slot), else a protected hour;
consistent daily beats heroic weekends (the J-curve, Ch 6 family). The 1-hour law is a
floor, not a ceiling — same skeleton as the Perfect Week's daily blocks.

**02 — Why knowledge compounds** — Buffett: knowledge compounds like interest (verified:
he reports 5–6 hours of reading a day; the "500 pages a day" quote is embellished —
Quote Investigator couldn't verify; a 2010 WSJ profile said 500 pages a WEEK — be honest).
Mechanism: prior knowledge raises the yield of new knowledge (each hour's return grows).

**03 — The skill currency** — The one-person economy (Ch 28 preview): skills are portable,
market-priced, and inflation-resistant; deliberate practice's honest variance (Macnamara:
huge in games/music, small in professions) means choose the domain by leverage, then log
hours. "You're not a genius or a failure; you're a compounding account."

**04 — The learner's life** — The identity frame (Ch 8): being a learner is a verb set
(study → apply → teach), not a credential. The teaching loop: learn → use at work → share
(one note, one post) — sharing is how knowledge becomes both retained and visible.

**05 — The reset trap** — Why ambitious resets fail: they borrow motivation (Ch 19) and
schedule intensity instead of consistency; the one-hour law's boringness is its protection.
The lag honesty: first year looks like nothing (Ch 6/29).

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: one domain per season; compounding is real; honest
Buffett; learner identity = verbs; consistency beats resets. Apply: pick the season's one
domain; book the daily hour; learn-then-share once this week; read one book straight through.
