## Sections
**Overview** — Hyperfocus as a trainable stack of four moves: clarity targeting, environment
lockdown, timeboxing, and the perfect week. Attention as the day's only real currency
(Ch 1's filter, upgraded to an operating budget).

**01 — Clarity targeting** — The target must be an output, not an activity: "finish the
proposal's section 2" not "work on proposal." Written target changes the filter (goal
priming: what you specify, you see — gorilla family). One target per block; vague blocks
attract drift.

**02 — Environment lockdown** — The environment does the focusing: phone in another room,
notifications off, tabs closed, one window, paper for stray thoughts. Interruptions cost
real time and stress (Mark — honest framing, no 23-minute claim); friction engineering
(Ch 18 family) is focus's silent partner.

**03 — Timeboxing** — Attention needs a frame: 25–90 minute boxes with a written output
checklist (Winner's Loop synergy); Parkinson's-law spirit (work expands to fill time —
mention as proverb, not law). Boxes end on schedule: the loop closes (plan-making
discharge, Ch 23 family) and the next box is fresh.

**04 — The perfect week** — The week is the unit of focus (Ch 14/27 preview): flow-aligned
blocks, admin windows, floors protected, slack kept. Hyperfocus inside a chaotic week is
heroics; the week does the engineering.

**05 — The honest limits** — Attention fatigues (no tank metaphors — just: quality degrades
over long sessions; schedule important work early — the Danziger pattern, honestly framed
from Ch 17). Wandering will occur (~47% baseline — Killingsworth & Gilbert); return-speed
is the skill (Ch 21's policy).

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: output targeting; environment does the work;
timeboxes close loops; week-level engineering; return-speed. Apply: write tomorrow's one
target as an output; lock down two environments (desk + phone); run one timeboxed block;
schedule admin windows; protect the week skeleton.
