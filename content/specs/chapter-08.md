## Sections
**Overview** — The stack: PHYSIOLOGY → STATE → IDENTITY → LANGUAGE → REALITY. Change
at the top without the bottom and it collapses. The stack is built from floor to ceiling,
five layers, each section covers one.

**01 — Physiology: the floor** — Sleep (Dawson/Reid direction: deprivation degrades cognition
and self-control), movement, breath. Body state biases which states are reachable — you don't
reason your way to courage on four hours of sleep. Keep the honest framing: <7h ≠ drunk; the
direction is real.

**02 — State: the room you act from** — Sleep/exercise compose the state; state selects
behavior. Borrowed-state preview: posture shifts felt power (Ranehill: feelings, not hormones —
be honest). State management is not spiritual; it's environmental + behavioral.

**03 — Identity: the operating system** — "I am not the kind of person who..." Identity as
filters for decisions: identity questions ("who would do this?"). James Clear's vote metaphor:
every action is a vote for the identity you're becoming; one vote never decides an election.

**04 — Language: the program output** — The words you use predict and shape behavior: "I can't"
vs "I haven't yet"; "I have to" vs "I choose to." Language is behavior too (it feeds the
self-fulfilling loop). Note the negativity-bias evidence (bad talk outweighs good) without the
fake 40x ratio.

**05 — Reality: what the stack produces** — When floor-through-language are aligned and
repeated, reality follows as measurement, not magic. Behavioral activation spine: act first,
identity follows (Dimidjian et al. — action precedes and generates motivation).

**06 — Building the stack in 20 minutes** — The Identity Stack Protocol: verbs not nouns;
70% check on each behavior; zero-day floor; do-then-tell; weekly review. Full protocol body at
The Identity Stack.

**Key Ideas / Apply Today** — Key ideas: five layers; physiology under everything; votes; verbs
not nouns; do-then-tell. Apply: define target identity as three behaviors; run the 70% check;
set the floor; do one behavior before announcing anything; schedule the weekly review.
